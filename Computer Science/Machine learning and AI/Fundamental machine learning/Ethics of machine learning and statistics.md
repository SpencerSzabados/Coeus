
# Formal non-discrimination criteria 
From [[@Hardt_2022]], the main idea of non-discrimination (or fairness) criteria is to introduce a discrete random variable $A$ that encodes membership status in one or multiple protested classes (hidden ? ). The random variable $Q$ lives in same probability space as the other covariates, $X$, with the decision rule $\hat{Y}=\mathbb{1}\{g\geq t\}$ in terms of a score $R$, and the outcome $Y$. Where $A$ might coincide with one of the features in $X$ or correlate strongly with some combination of them.

Statistical fairness criteria, in general, attempt to equalize group-dependent statistical quantity across the groups defined by the formulation of $A$, .e.g., for two groups $a$ and $b$, we have the imposed constraint $$P(\hat{Y}=1|A=a)=P(\hat{Y}=1|A=b),$$that must be satisfied by our derived decision (rule (predictor) $\hat{Y}$.
