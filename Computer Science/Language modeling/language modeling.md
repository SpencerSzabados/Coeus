A language model, in essence, attempts to gauge how probable a given string of text is to occur based on the context surrounding it. As the context sounding a word or sentence is important for its construction (and accuracy) language modeling is a [[Sequential learning]] problem. A language model, typically, is not structured to learn the specific grammar of a language but rather common conversational speech. [towardsdatascience](https://towardsdatascience.com/the-beginners-guide-to-language-models-aa47165b57f9), [deepset.ai](https://www.deepset.ai/blog/what-is-a-language-model), [medium](https://medium.com/@phylypo/a-survey-of-the-state-of-the-art-language-models-up-to-early-2020-aba824302c6).

Language modeling is typically used to describe models used for generating text (sentences) whereas [[Natural language processing]] refers to methods of extracting meaning (actionable information) from text (or speech). Language models are often used in natural language processing methods.
