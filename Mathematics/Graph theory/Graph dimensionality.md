There are number of different ways the dimensionality of a graph is measured, or interpreted, one of which is built around embedding a graph into the lowest dimensional space that allows all vertices be distinct (non-overlapping) with unit length edges.

**Definition: (Graph Euclidean dimension)** The Euclidean dimension of a graph $G$, denoted $e(G)$, is the smallest value of $d\geq 0$ of Euclidean $\mathbb{E}^d$ space in which $G$ can be embedded with every edges having unit length and every vertex distinct (no overlapping vertices) with allowance for edge crossings and edges overlapping vertices they are not incident to.

The Euclidean dimension of a complete graph $K_n$ is characterised by a the following theorem by Erdos(1965).

**Theorem:** The Euclidean dimension of $K_n$ is $e(K_n)=n-1$.
