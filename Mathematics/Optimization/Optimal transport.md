
# Overview 
Much of the following is taken from [[@Villani_2003]].

**Problem statement: (Optimal transport)** Consider two probability [[Measure spaces#^7ac46e|measures]] $\mu$ and $v$ defined on measure spaces $X$ and $Y$ respectively. Define the cost of applying a transformation that bring unit mass $x$ from $\mathcal{X}$ to $\mathcal{Y}$ by a cost function $L:X\times Y\to\mathbb{R}$. The question of optimal transport is to determine the transformation $T:X\to Y$ that minimizes the cost $L$ across all of $X$. More specifically, as it is more convent to represented $T$ by a probably measure $\pi:X\times Y\to [0,1]$, where $d\pi(x,y)$ measures the amount of mass transferred from location $x$ to location $y$. Then we are trying to fine $\pi$ that minimizes the quantity $$I[\pi] = \int_{X\times Y}c(x,y)\,d\pi(x,y)$$subject to the constraint that $$\int_X d\pi(x,y) = dv(y) \quad\text{ and }\quad \int_Yd\pi(x,y)=d\mu(x);$$or more specifically, $\pi(A\times Y)=\mu(A)$ and $\pi(X\times B)=v(B)$; this can also be seen as requiring $\pi$ to have $\mu$ and $v$ as marginals.

Optimal transport can be stated in term of [[Expected values#Expectation|expectation]] as $$I[U,V]=E_{(U,V)}[L(U,V)]$$for random variables $U$ and $V$ in $X$ nd $Y$ respectively with the given measures, and $\pi$ is the joint measure $\pi(u,v)$.


# Optimal transport of push forward measure
There exist a more restricted version of optimal transport, that results from Monge transforms, that requires that "no mass be split" in the transformation (transport plan) meaning $$d\pi(x,y) = d\mu(x)\delta\{y=T(x)\}$$where $T:X\to Y$ is a measurable map as originally described above. Under this restriction, and the conditions placed on the form of $\pi$ (also see [StackExchange](https://math.stackexchange.com/q/3108081)), $$I[\pi] = \int_{X\times Y}c(x,y)\,d\pi(x,y)=\int_X c(x,T(x))\,d\mu(x).$$In fact, the above restriction of $\pi(A\times Y)=\mu(A)$ and $\pi(X\times B)=v(B)$ can be translated into the condition that $$\int_X(\phi\circ T)\,d\mu = \int_Y\phi\,dv$$for all non-negative $\phi$, or equivalently the [[Push-forwards models#^3b91f2|push-forwards measure]] $$v(B) = \mu(T^{-1}(B))$$holds for all $B\subseteq Y$. Putting this all together, Monge's optimal transport problem seeks to minimize $$I[T] = \int_Xc(x,T(x))\, d\mu(x)$$over all measurable maps $T$ that pushforward $\mu$ to $v$, i.e., $T_\sharp \mu = v$.
