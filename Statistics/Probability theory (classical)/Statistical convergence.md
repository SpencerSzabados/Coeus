There are various notions of convergence used in statistics that spawn from notions in [[measure theory]] and [[Information theory]]. Typically, when referring to convergence in the statistical setting, we are interested in the limiting behaviour of a sequence $\{X_i\}$ of random variables which each follow from probability distribution; this problem is also known as stochastic convergence. The limiting quantity we are interested in, since we are dealing with random variables, may be many different things e.g., [[Expected values|expected value]], if the probability distribution changes over the course of the sequence we may be interested in knowing if the underlying distribution stabilizes on a specific form, or the probability of a certain event, etc... Hence there being more than one notion of convergence.
