Probabilistic graph models (PGMs), which hail from the domains of [[machine learning]] and [[Statistical learning]] (as of [2022] these kinds of models have somewhat fallen out of of favor as a result of the serge of deep learning methods), are statistical models that encode joint multivariant probabilities into graphs. See [TowardsDataScience](https://towardsdatascience.com/introduction-to-probabilistic-graphical-models-b8e0bf459812).

There are two primary classes of PGMs used: Directed Graphical Models (DGMs) (e.g., Bayesian Networks (BNs)), and Undirected Graphical Models (UGMs) or Markov Random Fields (MRFs) which relate to [[Markov chains]]. A common example of a MRF is a [[Markov chains|Hidden Markov Model]] (HMM) which describes a dynamic systems with periodic observations.
