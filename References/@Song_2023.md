The authors propose a method of distilling (or self teaching) [[Diffusion models]] to perform one step sampling by enforcing a property called _self consistency_. The authors apply this method to image generation tasks and show it maintains competitive performance wrt existing methods while being several times faster. 


# Primary results 
The authors begin by outlining the common diffusion equations used, namely: Let $p_0(x)$ denote the data distribution, $\mu$ and $\sigma$ be smooth scalar functions, and consider the [[Diffusion processes]] over $T>0$, $t\in[0,T]$ defined by the [[Stochastic differential equations]] $$dx_t = \mu(x_t,t)dt+\sigma(t)d\overrightarrow{W}_t;$$
with the simplifying function selection of $\mu(x_t,t)=0$ and $\sigma(t)=\sqrt{2t}$ being made so that the limiting distribution is $\pi=\mathcal{N}(0,T^2\mathbb{I})$. The backwards SDE is then of the form $$\frac{dx_t}{dt} = -t\log p_t(x_t) \approx -ts_\theta(x_t,t)$$where $s_\theta$ is the learned score network.

The authors then introduce the notion of _self consistency_, which is the focal point of the proposed method.

**Definition: (Consistent function)** Given a [[Ordinary differential equations|ODE]] of the form $$dx_t = g(x_t,t)dt$$with $g:\mathbb{R}^d\times [0,T]$ that is Lipschitz in $x$, and a sequence of points $(x_t)_{t=\epsilon}^T$ that lie along a single ODE trajectory $\tau(x_0,t)$, then a function $f:\tau(x_0,t)\to \tau(x_0,t)$, with imposed boundary condition $f(x_t,\epsilon)=x_\epsilon$ for $0<\epsilon<T$, is said to be _consistent_ if $$f(x_t,t)=f(x_{t'}, t')$$for all $t,t'\in [\epsilon, T]$. 

The consistency property enforces that any point along the ODE trajectory will be mapped back to some fixed point $x_\epsilon$ at the start of the trajectory; as the condition enforces the function is constant along the entire trajectory and equal to the imposed boundary condition. 

Then taking the PF-ODE of the above SDE $$dx_t = \bigg[\mu(x_t,t)-\frac{1}{2}\sigma(t)^2\nabla\log p_t(x_t)\bigg]dt$$ as the ODE the above definition gives us the proposed idea; during training we will enforce the model $f_\theta$ to obey this consistency criteria and modify the network using skip connections to ensure the boundary condition is satisfied. Namely, the authors follow [[@KarrasAAL_2022]] and include skip connections within the model such that $$f_\theta(x,t)=c_{skip}(t)x+c_{out}(t)D_\theta(x,t)$$
where $D_\theta$ is a network trained to be a denoisier with the modified [[@KarrasAAL_2022]] coefficients $$\begin{align}c_{skip}=\frac{s^2}{(t-\epsilon)^2+s^2}\quad{ and }\quad  c_{out}=\frac{s(t-\epsilon)}{\sqrt{s^2+t^2}}\end{align}$$where $s$ is selected to be the variance of the data distribution (or tuned) and obey the boundary conditions $c_{skip}(\epsilon)=1$ and $c_{out}(\epsilon)=0$.

The authors give to methods for training, one using distillation and the other self conditioning. The first method of training, which appears to be more stable to train and produces higher quality results, relies on distillation from a pre-trained diffusion model. 

```pseudo
\begin{algorithm}
\caption{Consistency Distillation}
\begin{algorithmic}
\Input Let $D$ be the dataset, $\{t_i\}_{i=1}^N$ be a discretization of the time horizon, $\eta$ the learning rate, $\delta$ the loss tolerance, $\mu$ the EMA decay rate, $f_\phi$ a pre-trained diffusion model (with score $s_\phi$) and $f_\theta$ be the initialised student model.
\Output Trained diffusion model $f_\theta$.

    \While{$L > \delta$}
        \State Sample $x\sim D$
        \State Sample $i\sim \mathcal{U}\{2,\dots,N\}$
        \State Sample $x_{t_i}\sim \mathcal{N}(x, t^2_i\mathbb{I})$\Comment{Forward SDE.}
        \State $\hat{x}_{t_{i-1}} \gets x_{t_i} - (t_{i-1}-t_i)t_is_\phi(x_{t_i},t_i)$ \Comment{ Euler step. }
        \State $L \gets \lambda(t_{i-1})d(f_\theta(x_{t_i},t_i),f_{\overline{\theta}}(\hat{x}_{t_{i-1}},t_{i-1}))$
        \State $\theta \gets \theta - \eta\nabla_\theta L$
        \State $\overline{\theta}\gets \text{stopgrad}(\mu\overline{\theta}+(1-\mu)\theta)$
    \EndWhile
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}
```

The authors give the following theorem for the training loss of this algorithm.

**Theorem: (Training loss)** Let $\Delta t=\max\{|t_i-t_j|\}$ and $f$ a solution to the consistency condition for the backwards SDE. Assume $f_\theta$ has Lipschitz constant $L>0$ in terms of $x$, and the ODE solver (e.g., Euler step) has local error uniformly bounded by $O((t_i-t_{i-1})^{p+1})$ with $p\geq 1$. Then, if $L=0$, or equivalently $\overline{\theta}=\theta$, we have $$\sup_{x,i}\|f_\theta(x,t_{i})-f(x,t_{i})\|_2=O(\Delta t^p).$$
The second method of training proposed by the authors is based on the (commonly used) unbiased estimator of the score function which they derive (not discover) for the simplifying drift and diffusion coefficients chosen. Namely, they use the one sample estimator  $$\begin{align}\nabla_x\log p_t(x_t) &= -\mathbb{E}_{x_0\sim p_0}\bigg[\frac{x_t-x_0}{t^2}\bigg| x_t\bigg]\\ &\approx -\frac{1}{t^2}D_\theta(x_t,t)\tag{Denoiser model.}\end{align}$$for performing the ODE integration step (e.g., Euler) in the training algorithm rather than using the teacher network.

```pseudo
\begin{algorithm}
\caption{Consistency Training}
\begin{algorithmic}
\Input Let $D$ be the dataset, $\{t_i\}_{i=1}^N$ be a discretization of the time horizon, $\eta$ the learning rate, $\delta$ the loss tolerance, $\mu$ the EMA decay rate, and $f_\theta$ be the initialised student model.
\Output Trained diffusion model $f_\theta$.

    \While{$L > \delta$}
        \State Sample $x\sim D$
        \State Sample $i\sim \mathcal{U}\{2,\dots,N\}$
        \State Sample $z\sim \mathcal{N}(0,\mathbb{I})$ \Comment{Noise sample.}
        \State $x_{t_i} \gets x + t_i \cdot z$
        \State $\hat{x}_{t_{i-1}} \gets x_{t_i} - \frac{t_i - t_{i-1}}{t_i}D_\theta(x_{t_i}, t_i)$

        \State $L \gets \lambda(t_{i-1})d\left(f_\theta(x_{t_i}, t_i), f_{\overline{\theta}}(\hat{x}_{t_{i-1}}, t_{i-1})\right)$
        \State $\theta \gets \theta - \eta\nabla_\theta L$
        \State $\overline{\theta} \gets \text{stopgrad}(\mu\overline{\theta} + (1 - \mu)\theta)$
    \EndWhile
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}
```
