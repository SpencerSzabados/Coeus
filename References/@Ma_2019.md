# Overview
This paper is focused on analyzing the (non-asymptotic) [[Monte Carlo simulation#Markov chain mixing time|mixing time]] and consequently the convergence towards optimizers of a locally non-[[Convex functions|convex function]] using a version of the [[Monte Carlo simulation#Metropolis algorithm|Metropolis algorithm]], specifically the Metropolis Adjusted Langevian Algorithm (given below). These results are then contrasted against those for traditional optimization methods for (locally) [[Quasi-convex functions]]. It is shown that sampling based optimization methods (i.e., certain [[Stochastic optimization]] methods) are faster for the particular class of functions considered that standard optimization methods.

The particular optimization problem studied herein concerns a class of functions that are [[Convex functions#Strong convexity|strongly convex]] (in particular strongly [[s-Concave densities#Log-concave densities|log-concave]]) outside of a bounded region (which may or may not be convex itself) while allowed to be non-convex within the region itself. In particular:

**Problem statement:** Let $p$ be a target distribution (e.g., $E_p[x]$ is the sought objective value) with $p\propto e^{-U}$ for some function $U$ that is $m$-strongly convex outside of a region $B_R(x_0)$ of radius $R$ and is $L$-[[Lipschitz continuity|lipschitz]] smooth (i.e., $\|\nabla U(x)-\nabla U(y)\|\leq L\|x-y\|$ for all $x,y$ outside of the region). Moreover, let $k=L/m$ be the _condition number_ of $U$, which measures how similar $U$ it to the isotropic (e.g., [[Distributions along projections#^a2d68c|C-isotopic]]) quadratic function outside of the region. Then given an error tolerance $0<\epsilon <1$ and an initial distribution $p_0$ we want to know the mixing-time with respect to total variation for convergence to $p$. 


# Primary results 
The paper is focused on the Metropolis Adjusted Lagevian Algorithm (below) which makes use of the reparameterization trick to incorporate randomization into a gradient decent method with rejection sampling.

```pseudo
\begin{algorithm}
\caption{Metropolis Adjusted Langevian Algorithm}
\begin{algorithmic}
\Input Target distribution $p$ defined in terms of objective function $U$, an initial starting point $x_0$, initial distribution $p_0=\mathcal{N}(0,\frac{1}{L}I)$ (Isotropic normal), temperature schedule $\beta_t$, and error tolerance $0<\epsilon<1$. 
\Output Approximate minimizer $x$ of $U$ or equivalently a distribution $p_T$ with total variation $\delta(p_T,p)<\epsilon$.

\While{$t=1,2,\dots,T$}
    \State Update the temperature schedu le$\beta_t$
    \State Draw sample $dw\sim \mathcal{N}(0,2\beta_tI)$
    \State Draw sample $dn\sim \mathcal{N}(0,1)$
    \State Draw sample point $x_{t+1} \gets x_t-\beta_t\nabla U(x_t)+dw$ 
    \State\Comment{Perform rejection sampling:}
    \State Let $n(x|y)=\mathcal{N}(x_t-\beta_t\nabla U(x_t),1)$
    \If{$\frac{n(x_t|x_{t+1})p(x_t)}{n(x_{t+1}|x_t)p(x_{t+1})}<dn$}
        \State $x_{t+1}\gets x_t$
    \EndIf
\EndWhile
\Return $x_T$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}
```

**Theorem: (Convergence)** For initial distribution $p_0=\mathcal{N}(0,\frac{1}{L}I)$ and error tolerance $0<\epsilon<1$. For temperature schedule $\beta_t\in O(\frac{\epsilon}{kdLe^{16LR^2}})$ the unadjusted Langevin algorithm converges in total variation to $p\propto e^{-U}$ within $T\in O(\frac{dk^2}{\epsilon^2}\ln(\frac{d}{\epsilon^2})e^{32LR^2})$ steps. For temperature schedule $\beta_t\in O(\frac{1}{\sqrt{dk}\sqrt{d\ln(k)+\ln(1/\epsilon)}Le^{8LR^2}})$ converges in total variation in $T\in O(\frac{\sqrt{d}k^{3/2}}{m}(d\ln(k)+\ln(1/\epsilon))^{3/2}e^{40LR^2})$ many steps.


# Connections 
This paper is closely connected to [[@Dwivedi_2018]] which is more focused on analyzing the mixing time of the MALA for [[s-Concave densities#Log-concave densities|log-concave density]] sampling.

The MALA can be seen as a form of diffusion process so under view this paper is related to [[@Stroock_1972]] [[@Chiang_1987]] both of which study the optimization of convex functions using diffusion methods.
