# Overview
Few shot learning (for [[Classification and clustering]]) is the problem of training networks that perform well given comparatively little data, with respect to how much data other currently used methods require to perform tasks effectively, with emphasis to generalization performance to unseen classes. 

In particular, given an abundant training data of the from $\{(X_b,Y_b)\}_{b=1}^M$ dealing with a base number of known classes and a small amount of training data for some novel classes $\{(X_n,Y_n)\}_{n=1}^N$, the goal of few shot learners is to accurately classify inputs originating from novel classes while primarily being trained using this based class information and the small collection of novel class information.

The paper focuses on three kinds of few-shot learning methods, _initialization based methods_, _distance learning methods_, and so called _hallucination based methods_.

## Initialization based methods
Initialization based methods aim to find good initial model parameter values to accelerate training and convergence when limited training data is available. These methods, according to the paper authors, do not deal with domain shifts effectively.


## Distance metric methods (meta learning)
The intuition behind this method is to learn how to compare observations based on some measure of similarity between them. New observations can then be assessed based on their distance (similarity) to known data. 


## Hallucination based methods
Hallucination methods are based on the idea of learning generating functions (for generating new examples) for the underlying prediction classes and then extrapolating other potential classes based on the learned generators.


# Primary results
This paper primarily survey as a method survey and critic of different benchmarking methods and how to incorporate data domain shift (different training and sample data) into testing.
