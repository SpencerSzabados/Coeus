# Overview
This paper proposes an extenuation to the method from [[@Lipman_2023]] to enable it to operate on [[Riemannian manifolds]] as well as (some) simplicial manifolds, of the variety used in computer graphics, for the generation of [[Probability paths]] (e.g., texture generation, maze solving, etc.). The authors develop the results of this paper by making a few simplifying assumptions on the form of probability [[Linear dynamical systems#Flows|flow]] considered; namely, the authors assume access to, what they call, a "pre-metric", or what is better called a surrogate metric which may not obey the triangle inequality; they also assume the ode flow constructed admits a unique solution and is therefore learnable by a [[Artificial neural networks]].

# Primary results
The goal of this paper is to construct a method of effectively learning a ode flow $\psi:[0,1]\times \mathcal{M}\to \mathcal{M}$ atop a smooth connected riemannian manifold $(\mathcal{M}, g)$ of the form $$\frac{d}{dt}\psi_t(x)=u_t(\psi_t(x)),\quad \psi_0(x)=x$$and constructing a probability path $q_t$, by learning $u_t$ above, that transports $p_0$, which is known, to $p_1$, of which only an empirical estimate is known, via $$\begin{align}\log q_t(x) &=\log({\psi_t}_{\sharp}p_0)\\ &=\log(p_0(\psi_t^{-1}(x)))-\int_0^t\text{div}_g(u_t)(x_s)\, ds.\end{align}$$
This is formalized by the vector filed minimization objective $$\begin{cases}&\min_{v_\theta\in T_x\mathcal{M}} \mathbb{E}_{t\sim\mathcal{U}[0,1], X\sim p_t}[\|u_t(x)-v_\theta(x,t)\|_g^2]\\& s.t., p_0 = q_0 \text{ and } p_1\approx q_1 \end{cases}$$where $v_\theta$ is a vector field parameterized by a neural network and $u_t$ is pre-defined vector field that, through some simplifying assumptions, can be computed in closed form. At present, we cant sample from $p_t$ so must chose a model parametrization that allows evaluation of the above loss function. the authors opt to look at per-samples conditional probability paths $p_t(x|x_1)$ with the boundary conditions $$p_0(x|x_1)=p_0(x) \text{ and } p_1(x|x_1)\approx \delta\{x-x_1\}$$allowing the marginalization $$p_t(x) = \int_\mathcal{M} p_t(x|x_1)p_1(x_1)\, d\omega(x_1),$$where $d\omega$ is the riemannian volume element. The authors then define $$u_t(x)=\int_\mathcal{M}u_t(x|x_1)\frac{p_t(x|x_1)p_1(x_1)}{p_t(x)}\, d\omega(x_1);$$however, this remains intractable as we do not have access to $p_1$.

## Method
Till now we have just stated the generalization of flow matching to manifolds, but have not discussed how to compute the objective. 

The authors get around this by considering a conditional flow $\psi_t(x_0|x_1)$ where $$\begin{cases}\frac{d}{dt}\psi_t(x_0|x_1)=u_t(\psi_t(x_0|x_1)|x_1)\\ \psi_0(x_0|x_1)=x_0\\\psi_1(x_|x_1)=x_1,\end{cases}$$and every point flow towards $x_1$, under which the initial objective reduces to $$\min_{v_\theta\in T_x\mathcal{M}} \mathbb{E}_{t\sim\mathcal{U}[0,1], x_1\sim p_1, X_0\sim p_0}[\|u_t(x_t|x_1)-v_\theta(x_t,t)\|_g^2]$$and can be tractably computed provided $u_t$ can be evaluated efficiently. The central contribution of this paper is in the construction of an efficient methods of evaluating $u_t$ on manifolds; in particular, given a distance metric surrogate $d(x,y)$, and a monotonically decreasing differentiable function $\kappa:[0,1]\to [0,1]$ with $\kappa(0)=1$ and $\kappa(1)=0$ the authors, assume the existence of, a flow $$d(\psi_t(x_0|x_1),x_1)=\kappa(t)d(x_0,x_1)$$which gives rise to the vector filed equation $$u_t(x|x_1) = \frac{d\log \kappa(t)}{dt}d(x,x_1)\frac{\nabla_xd(x,x_1)}{\|\nabla_xd(x,x_1)\|_g^2}.$$
In order to evaluate $d$ atop $\mathcal{M}$ without access to the true geodesics, which may be to expensive to compute, the authors use the eigen-function approximation $$d(x,y)^2=\sum_{i=1}^k w(\lambda_i)(\phi_i(x)-\phi_i(y))^2$$based on the eigen-value-function pairs $(\nabla\cdot\nabla)_\mathcal{M}\psi_i = \lambda_i \phi_i$ of the [[Laplace-Beltrami operator]].

# Experimental results
The proposed method performs well across the various manifold environments tested within the paper, and is a flexible framework that can easily be adapted to different manifolds thanks to the distance approximation used, moreover, the method scales more favourably (in terms of memory usage and accuracy) in dimension than competing Riemann manifold score matching methods.

# Issues
This method has one, or perhaps two, issues, ignoring the question of the well-posed nature of a solution, in that the norm of the vector field $u_t$ does not anneal towards the origin pole. This means that sampling near $x_0$ is sensitive to perturbations or numerical error. More specifically, under $k(t)=1-t$, but I believe the following results holds more generally, we get $$u_t(x|x_1)=d(x_0,x_1)\frac{\nabla_xd(x_t,x_1)}{\|\nabla_xd(x_t,x_1)\|_g^2}.$$Where at $t= 0$ the magnitude of the vector field is actually maximal with $\|u_0(x|x_1)\|_g^2=d(x_0,x_1)$; unlike diffusion, which anneals in norm, due to the noise component, to zero.
